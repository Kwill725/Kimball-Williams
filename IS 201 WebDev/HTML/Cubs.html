Party Like it's 2016

From loveable losers to World Series champions

The Early Years

The Chicago Cubs, established in 1876, are one of the oldest and most storied franchises in Major League Baseball. Known for their passionate fan base and historic Wrigley Field, the Cubs have experienced both highs and lows throughout their history.

The Curse of the Billy Goat

One of the most infamous aspects of the Cubs' history is the "Curse of the Billy Goat," which allegedly began in 1945 when a tavern owner named Billy Sianis was asked to leave Wrigley Field because his pet goat's odor was bothering other fans. Sianis supposedly declared that the Cubs would never win another World Series, a curse that seemed to hold true for decades.

The 2016 World Series Victory

After more than a century of heartbreak, the Chicago Cubs finally broke the curse in 2016. The team was built by Theo Epstein, the General Manager that broke the curse for the 2004 Red Sox. Theo acquired star players like Kris Bryant, Anthony Rizzo, John Lester, and Ben Zobrist (Who eventually won the 2016 World Series MVP). After the 2015 season ended in the NLCS against the Mets, the Cubs defeated the Cleveland Indians in a thrilling seven-game series to win their first World Series title since 1908. The victory was celebrated by fans worldwide and marked a historic moment in baseball history.

Legacy and Impact

The Cubs' 2016 championship not only ended a long-standing drought but also revitalized the franchise and its fan base. The team's success has inspired a new generation of fans and solidified the Cubs' place as one of baseball's most beloved teams.
